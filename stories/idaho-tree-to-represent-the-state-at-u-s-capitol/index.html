Idaho tree to represent the state at U.S. Capitol

Idaho is sending a piece of Christmas spirit to Washington, D.C. this holiday season. A 76-foot tall Engelmann Spruce from the Nez Perce-Clearwater National Forest has been selected as this year’s U.S. Capitol Christmas tree.

By Jack McElwee · The Arbiter

Idaho is sending a piece of Christmas spirit to Washington, D.C. this holiday season.

A 76-foot tall Engelmann Spruce from the Nez Perce-Clearwater National Forest has been selected as this year’s U.S. Capitol Christmas tree.

The selection process took more than a year and began with U.S. Forest Service workers scouting trees in the selected forest with suitable shapes.

“Ultimately, this tree was selected by the architect at the Capitol’s office in Washington D.C.,” Zach Peterson, staff officer with the Nez Perce-Clearwater National Forest, said. “They sent the Executive Director of Grounds and Arboretum out, and we showed him 12 trees. He liked this one.”

The tree is set to stand in the U.S. Capitol’s West Lawn where an Idaho fourth grader will help light it during an arrival ceremony in December.

Boise Cascade was chosen to transport the tree from Idaho to Washington, D.C. The Boise-headquartered company will transport the tree on a specially built trailer, with four drivers taking turns behind the wheel throughout the journey.

“It didn’t take long for us to accept,” Rebecca Troescher, senior communications specialist at Boise Cascade, said. “It’s a tremendous honor to be chosen, not only to participate, but have the responsibility of getting the tree there.”

The “People’s Tree” is a national tradition which originated in 1964 when Speaker of the House, John W. McCormack suggested a tree be planted on the Capitol grounds.

After the original tree died in 1967 after a snowstorm, the Architect of the Capitol turned to the forest service to provide a replacement. Since then, a different national forest supplies the tree each year.

This year’s tree is touring towns and cities across the country on its way to the nation’s capitol. Idaho stops include Twin Falls, Idaho Falls and Craters of the Moon National Monument.

At Craters of the Moon, a live band and a Smoky the Bear mascot welcomed the tree.

A window on the back of the tree’s trailer gave onlookers a first-hand look at the Idaho-inspired ornaments decorating the tree. Markers and ladders were also available for people to sign the banner covering the trailer.

Among the spectators, one sentiment was consistent: Idahoans are proud the tree is coming from their state.

“It’s a proud tradition, but to have it be on the 250th [anniversary], it’s a big deal,” said Matthew Conde, public affairs director for AAA Idaho. “Obviously they’ve chosen a different National Forest every year for the last 56 [years] so it is a big deal. We got very lucky.”

Conde and AAA are providing emergency roadside support for the tree’s convoy as it makes its way east.

“It’s just one of those things that when you live in Idaho, you’ve got to go see,” said Ed Millspaugh of West Yellowstone, Mont.

This is the third time Idaho has supplied the U.S. Capitol Christmas tree, following 2003 and 2016.

“For most of us employees, it’s once in a career opportunity,” Peterson said. “It’s just a really awesome opportunity to share a little bit about our forest and the spirit of Christmas, and share this tree with the whole country.”
