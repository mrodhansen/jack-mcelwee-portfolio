‘It’s in the name right?’ Opportunity zones bring possibility for significant campus development

The U.S. Treasury approved 25 opportunity zones in Idaho on Oct. 1, with five located in Boise and one covering the entirety of Boise State’s campus.

By Jack McElwee · The Arbiter

Boise State may be seeing increased development thanks to a federal program designed to foster growth.

The U.S. Treasury approved 25 opportunity zones in Idaho on Oct. 1, with five located in Boise and one covering the entirety of Boise State’s campus.

An opportunity zone is an area where private developers qualify for federal tax benefits which incentivize development on that land.

“This is really designed to encourage private investment in communities and areas of our city that are well positioned for growth,” Kiah Jones, Boise Mayor’s Office housing advisor said. “For Boise, we see this as a tool to really attract private investment into housing, business, commercial development, and redevelopment of underutilized properties.”

While the city recommends the zones, it does not own the land within them.

“The city will be involved when people come in and get permits for new development,” Jones said. “But for now, really, this is a tool to see what the market will bring to those areas.”

One of Boise’s opportunity zones encompasses the entirety of Boise State’s campus, stretching from Broadway Avenue to Capitol Boulevard, with the Boise River serving as the northern boundary and Boise Avenue marking the southern boundary.

Associate Vice President for Campus Operations Drew Alexander said there are no immediate plans to develop the land. However, he said the opportunity zones will be used as a tool as the university moves forward with its 10-Year Plan.

According to the 10-Year Plan, the university will need an additional 260,000 square feet of academic space by 2033.

“At a high level, [opportunity zones] could be a mechanism to support a lot of that,” Alexander said. “We just don’t have concrete plans or a blueprint, so to speak, of how we plan to deploy it or implement it right here in the moment.”

The zone designation could also play a role in the potential future development of Albertson Stadium’s east parking lot.

In a letter to the Boise State community titled “Project Announcement” President David W. Hahn said the university is exploring a potential development project in the east parking lot that would bring a significant economic impact to the city.

“Potential uses could include hotel, conference, event, retail, dining, housing and parking options,” Hahn wrote. “The final mix will be shaped by feasibility, community value and the potential to generate sustainable revenue.”

Alexander said opportunity zones would likely impact the development of this project by being a part of a potential financing plan should the project move forward.

“If [the east parking lot project] were to move forward, that I think would very likely be a component of the financing plan for a project like that,” Alexander said. “We are still well within just a feasibility phase on that project.”

Beyond the east parking lot project, the designation could also encourage other developments on or around campus — possibly in the form of residence halls.

“I would look at what higher education institutions have typically done under a public-private partnership delivery method,” Alexander said. “Most frequently you see student

housing being delivered in a public-private partnership.”

One of these public-private partnerships was used to build Kirwan-Blanding Hall at the University of Kentucky, a four-story, 649-bed residence hall that opened in August 2026. The residence hall was made possible through an $81 million dollar partnership with real estate development firm Greystar.

While specific development plans remain uncertain, the opportunity zones could provide new financial opportunities for future Boise developments. Alexander recommended looking at Boise State’s 10-Year Plan for future projects.

“We’re excited for the selection and the designation that’s been made official,” Alexander said. “And looking forward to seeing how the tool can be an opportunity. It’s in the name right?”
